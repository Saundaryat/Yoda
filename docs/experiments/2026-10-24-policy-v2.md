## What changed

The judge now receives explicit authoritative instructions and the full policy only when the agent configuration preloads it. Historical policy bytes are checked against the pre-run protocol's SHA-256. Available skills are not silently granted: only successful skill-view results preceding the reply in the same session supply policy evidence. A policy establishes rules, not completion of an action; booking, reservation, escalation, prices and availability still require earlier successful CRM evidence. Jev receives the same authority context for severity decisions.

All 120 saved conversations in both arms were fully rejudged, not selectively patched. Original task checks and mechanical CRM scoring remain unchanged. Each replay reuses exact event payloads, CRM states and Hermes session ids. All original scores and judge outputs were checked unchanged after publication. The corrected experiment is inserted atomically as finished, with every run already scored and attempts zero; no queue work is created. A snapshot audit verifies all 120 copies.

New Hermes calls: **0**. New simulated-customer calls: **0**. Estimated new judge/Jev replay cost: **$1.89**, excluding calibration and unreported failed-request usage. Costs do not double-charge the reused agent/customer work. The UI links corrected results to the original scores and original runs.

## Corrected results

| Pass rate | A: available | B: preloaded |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Overall | 60.0% (36/60) | 81.7% (49/60) |
| Policy | 16.7% (4/24) | 83.3% (20/24) |
| Controls | 83.3% (20/24) | 87.5% (21/24) |
| Context/memory | 100% (12/12) | 66.7% (8/12) |

Overall difference: **+21.7 percentage points**, 95% two-level bootstrap interval **−1.7 to +45.0 points**, sign-test p=0.2266. Eight scenarios favored B, three favored A, nine tied. Overall verdict remains **inconclusive**. The policy subgroup is descriptive, not an independently pre-powered confirmatory result. This suite was already used to choose the follow-up treatment; it is not an untouched holdout.

All three B same-day conversations now pass rather than failing for accurately quoting the supplied no-same-day rule. Guardian checks and booking notes also improve. Remaining real failures include service recall (A 3/3, B 0/3), an incorrect $140 price in one B cross-contact reply, an unsupported reservation promise, and incomplete lost-slot explanation. Neither arm passes callback-deadline handling reliably. Do not promote B solely from the aggregate improvement.

Original raw scores were A 40/60 and B 43/60. Full replay changed four A passes to failures and six B failures to passes; this is not simply adding three points to B. Some differences reflect newly explicit policy-evidence boundaries and some reflect judge sampling variation. The original calibration did not cover the supplied-policy distinction. An additional independent human review of score flips is warranted before treating model judgments as definitive.
